# Right Sharpa Wave hand on the Tianji Marvin M6 CCS end flange

`tianji_m6_ccs_sharpa_right` is the wrist bench's second arm: the Marvin Pro
torso with its right Marvin M6 CCS arm carrying the right Sharpa Wave hand, the
left arm welded at Tianji's home pose. It is generated by
`dex_teleop/scripts/build_tianji_sharpa_right.py` from the dual-arm
`tianji_m6_ccs` model already in `omnigibson-robot-assets` (Tianji/Gento's
`Marvin_Description`, see `source/tianji_m6_ccs/UPSTREAM.md`) and Sharpa's
official `right_sharpa_wave_with_flange` model, the same hand model and the
same flange adapter the ARAT Franka carries. This note records where the hand
sits on the arm and why, with the official sources, in the way
`franka_sharpa_right/source/WRIST_CAMERAS.md` does for the palm cameras.

## What Tianji publishes about the arm and its flange

Tianji's product pages and brochures for the Marvin series and the Marvin Pro
(the fixed dual-arm platform the `marvin_pro` meshes come from) give the arm's
geometry and its tool interface, but not a flange drawing:

| Working reach, **J2 axis to end flange** | **696 mm** | same |

The numeric flange geometry comes from two official engineering artefacts in
Tianji's SDK repository ([TJ_FX_ROBOT_CONTRL_SDK](https://github.com/cynthia-you/TJ_FX_ROBOT_CONTRL_SDK)):

1. **`CommonConfig/ccs_m6_40.MvKDCfg`** (branch `master`), the M6 4.0
   kinematic/dynamic configuration the Marvin SDK's kinematics library
   (`libKine`) loads. Each row is a modified-DH joint
   (`alpha, a, d, theta, lower, upper, vel, acc, mass, com, inertia`) and
   reproduces the URDF chain exactly (d1 = 174.5, d3 = 287, a4 = a5 = 18,
   d5 = 314 mm, the J2/J4 upper limits restricted for the humanlike elbow).
   Its final row is the **tool/flange frame: `alpha = 90, a = 0, d = 95 mm,
   theta = 90`**, i.e. the flange origin lies 95 mm from the J7 axis along
   Link7 `-Y`, with the same orientation pattern as the J6 and J7 frames
   (`Rx(90) Rz(90)`, the orientation the Gento `flange_R` frame already uses).
2. **`URDF/Marvin M6-CCS-696-urdf_V4.0.rar`** (branch `资料文档`), Tianji's
   own single-arm URDFs exported from Fusion. The right-arm file ends at
   `Link7_R` with no flange frame, but its `Link7_R.STL` is byte-identical to
   the `Arm_R7_Link.STL` in our asset and its flange face lies exactly at
   **`y = -0.095 m`**: the mesh carries the flange.

Both agree with the published reach: 287 + 314 + 95 = **696 mm** from the J2
axis to the end flange. The Gento description instead places its `flange_R`
frame (and the parallel gripper mounted on it) at 88 mm, 7 mm *inside* the
physical flange face (the gripper base mesh starts at that frame, so in the
upstream model the gripper is buried 7 mm in Link7; 287 + 314 + 88 = 689 mm
does not match the data sheet). The Sharpa hand is therefore mounted on the
official 95 mm frame and the Gento `flange_R` frame is dropped.

### The flange pattern, measured on the official mesh

A cross-section of `Link7_R.STL` 2 mm inside the flange face shows:

- four holes of ~5 mm diameter (M6 tap drill) on a **50.0 mm pitch circle at
  +-45 and +-135 degrees**;
- one ~6 mm hole on the same pitch circle at **0 degrees, along Link7 `+X`**:
  the locating-pin bore;
- a 32.4 mm centring recess at the face and a central ~10.6 mm cable
  pass-through (the arm's hollow wrist);
- a 62 mm face.

That is the ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6 interface (Ø50 PCD, 4 x M6, Ø6 pin, Ø31.5 H7
centring, Ø63 outer), the same standard interface as the Franka Emika flange
(DIN ISO 9409-1-A50) on which Sharpa's adapter is already mounted in
`franka_sharpa_right`. Sharpa's `flange_B.STL` adapter has the matching
geometry: four Ø6.5 clearance holes on a 50.0 mm pitch circle at +-45/+-135
degrees with counterbores on the hand side, a Ø31.5 pilot boss 2 mm tall on the
robot side, a Ø63 rim, and its pin hole on the pitch circle at 0 degrees, along
the adapter's **`+X`**. Franka's link7 flange shows the same four M6 holes on
Ø50 and its Ø6 pin hole at 90 degrees (link7 `+Y`).

## The mount in the URDF

Mating the adapter to the Tianji flange leaves one degree of freedom, the roll
about the flange normal, and the pin fixes it: adapter `+X` on Link7 `+X`. With
the adapter's `+Z` (the hand axis, `right_hand_flange -> right_hand_wrist ->
right_hand_C_MC` are translations along `+Z`) on the outward flange normal
(Link7 `-Y`):

```text
Arm_R7_Link --tianji_right_hand_flange_joint (fixed)--> right_hand_flange
            xyz = "0 -0.095 0"   rpy = "1.5707963 0 0"      (Rx(90) Rz(90) · Rz(-90) = Rx(90))
right_hand_flange --(0, 0, 0.0005)--> right_hand_wrist --(0, 0, 0.029)--> right_hand_C_MC --> fingers
```

Consequences in the Link7 frame: the hand axis (Sharpa `+Z`, wrist to
fingertips) is Link7 `-Y`; the palm normal (`right_hand_C_MC +X`) is Link7
`+X`; the pinky-to-thumb axis (`+Y`) is Link7 `+Z`, the J7 axis. So J7 is wrist
flexion/extension, J6 (axis = Link7 `+X`) radial/ulnar deviation and J5
pronation/supination, as on a human arm. In the zero configuration the arm
points straight out to the robot's right with the palm facing down.

Unlike the Franka merge, which rotated the hand so its palm normal lies along
the flange axis (a modelling convenience for the ARAT reset pose; the IK
absorbs the mount), this mount is the physical one. The two arms therefore
need different reset configurations, which is why the wrist bench keeps the
reset poses per arm in `hand_bench.yaml`.

## Reset pose

The bench's `extended` pose for this arm puts `right_hand_C_MC` at
`(0.50, -0.20, 1.10)` m in the `base_link` frame (whose origin is at the
torso's floor contact plane, so z is the height above the floor): straight in
front of the right shoulder (`Arm_R1` at `(0, -0.2005, 1.203)`), palm normal
straight down, fingers along `+X`, thumb toward `+Y`. It was solved with
`build_tianji_sharpa_right.py --solve-reset-pose` (Pinocchio damped least
squares, 120 random restarts, best joint-limit margin with the elbow below and
outside the shoulder):

```text
Arm_R1..R7 = [-1.233317, -1.338584, 1.411068, -1.857169, -1.387896, 0.215559, 0.585308]
elbow (Arm_R4) at (0.11, -0.26, 0.94), nearest joint limit 39 degrees away (J4)
```

With the bench robot at world `(-0.63, 0, 0)` the palm hovers at
`(-0.13, -0.20, 1.10)`: 0.35 m above the 0.75 m tabletop, over the rear edge of
the table in front of the robot's right shoulder, fingertips reaching the table
centre. The `home` pose is Tianji's upstream Marvin Pro home configuration
(`[-1.7, -1.1, 1.1, -2.0, 0.37, 0.13, -0.55]`): hand at `(0.37, -0.15, 0.945)`
in the base frame, palm facing inward, fingers forward.

## Other changes to the upstream model

- `base_link` is re-origined 82 mm down, at the underside of the base plate,
  so the OmniGibson importer's bottom-surface normalisation is a no-op (on the
  original `tianji_m6_ccs` import it shifted the links but not the joints
  attached to the root link, leaving the shoulders 82 mm below the torso's
  shoulder yoke). The shoulders stay at 1.203 m above the floor, where the
  torso mesh puts them.
- The torso's placeholder collision cylinder is replaced by box colliders for
  the base plate (0.754 x 0.754 x 0.082 m), column, shoulder yoke and head,
  measured on cross-sections of `ZJ_Robot_link.STL`.
- The left arm and gripper are welded at the upstream home pose (their joint
  values folded into fixed-joint origins), so the model is a single 7-DoF arm
  plus the 22 Sharpa finger joints: the same 28-D action (6-D IK pose + 22
  fingers) as `franka_sharpa_right`, with `SharpaActionAdapter` unchanged.
- Mass-less upstream frames (`tracking_base_link`, `flange_L`,
  `left_gripper_mount`, `left_tool`) get the 1 µg / 2e-12 kg m² placeholder
  Sharpa uses for its fingertip frames, so every articulation link has a
  positive mass.
- Joint limits are the upstream Gento ones (J2 and J4 upper limits at -0.1 rad
  instead of Tianji's +120/+60 degrees), the same as in `tianji_m6_ccs`.

## Rebuilding the asset

```bash
# 1. URDF, meshes, robot definition and importer configuration
python dex_teleop/scripts/build_tianji_sharpa_right.py
python dex_teleop/scripts/build_tianji_sharpa_right.py --solve-reset-pose   # optional check

# 2. URDF -> USD (Isaac Sim, several minutes, headless)
OMNIGIBSON_DATA_PATH=$PWD/datasets OMNI_KIT_ACCEPT_EULA=YES \
  python -m omnigibson.examples.robots.import_custom_robot \
  --config datasets/omnigibson-robot-assets/models/tianji_m6_ccs_sharpa_right/tianji_m6_ccs_sharpa_right.import.yaml

# 3. Move the importer's output next to the URDF
python dex_teleop/scripts/build_tianji_sharpa_right.py --finish-import
```

Then `python dex_teleop/scripts/launch_wrist_bench.py --arm tianji --view-only`.
